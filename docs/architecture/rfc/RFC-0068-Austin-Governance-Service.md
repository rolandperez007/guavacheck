# RFC-0068

# Austin Governance Service

**Status:** Draft v1.0  
**Category:** Enterprise Services Layer  
**System:** Austin Cognitive Operating System  
**Maintainer:** Guava Networks Limited

---

# Abstract

The Austin Governance Service (AGS) is the centralized policy enforcement system responsible for ensuring that every action performed by Austin complies with its Constitutional Laws, institutional policies, legal obligations, enterprise rules, and user permissions.

Where the Austin Kernel provides cognition, the Governance Service provides authority.

Nothing of significance occurs inside Austin without governance.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Governance Service provides:

- constitutional enforcement
- policy evaluation
- authorization
- compliance validation
- institutional governance
- execution approval
- audit support

---

# 2. Core Principle

Every significant action requires governance before execution.

```
Requested Action

↓

Governance Evaluation

↓

Approve

Reject

Escalate
``` id="governance-core"

Governance precedes execution.

---

# 3. Governance Layers

Austin evaluates actions against multiple layers.

### Layer 1

Austin Constitutional Laws

Highest authority.

---

### Layer 2

Platform Policies

Austin OS operational rules.

---

### Layer 3

Institutional Policies

Organization-specific rules.

---

### Layer 4

User Permissions

Identity-based authority.

---

### Layer 5

Workflow Constraints

Context-specific rules.

All layers participate in decision making.

---

# 4. Governance Decisions

Possible outcomes:

```
Approved

Rejected

Requires Human Review

Deferred

Limited Approval
```

Not every request receives a binary decision.

---

# 5. Constitutional Enforcement

The service verifies compliance with all Constitutional RFCs.

Examples:

- Reality Is Authoritative
- Knowledge Requires Provenance
- Explainability
- Human Authority Supremacy

Constitutional violations immediately terminate execution.

---

# 6. Policy Engine

Policies are represented as governed rules.

Example:

```
IF

Property Value > ₦500,000,000

THEN

Require Human Approval
```

Policies remain versioned and auditable.

---

# 7. Identity Integration

Governance queries the Identity Service to determine:

- permissions
- trust level
- organization
- delegated authority

Authority is never assumed.

---

# 8. Risk Assessment

Before approval Austin evaluates:

- operational risk
- financial risk
- legal risk
- privacy risk
- confidence level

Higher risk increases governance scrutiny.

---

# 9. Human Escalation

Certain actions automatically require humans.

Examples:

- land ownership transfer
- regulatory filings
- large financial commitments
- constitutional exceptions

Austin requests review rather than acting independently.

---

# 10. Institutional Governance

Organizations may define custom policies.

Examples:

- banking regulations
- government compliance
- insurance underwriting
- enterprise approval chains

Institutional policies extend—but never override—the Constitution.

---

# 11. Audit Trail

Every governance decision records:

```

↓

Decision

↓

↓

``` id="audit"

Governance becomes fully explainable.
